As someone who has lived in Bangalore all my life, I find this city offers several things to look forward to every year. One such annual constant on my calendar is the “Bangalore Literature Festival,” and I have made an effort to not miss it. I first came across this beautiful event when _The Hindu_ pitched it to me on a Sunday morning. Between my Bertie Ashley column and Mr Mathrubootham’s letter, on a typical Sunday morning, my eyes wandered across the topical featuring LitFest in great wonder. That, folks, is when I realised hey this sounds fun and I want to be there. Thus began my tantrum, much to my parents’ chagrin, to attend the event.

![Collage](/assets/ramblings/lit_collage.webp)

Over the years, the festival has grown bigger and better. The latest edition of the festival was no different. It brought the best of Bangalore’s reading community together for a three day event. From authors of nonfiction to stalwarts of translation, this year’s LitFest had something for everyone. Not to mention the kids’ exclusive “CLF,” dedicated to making the event all the more memorable for the readers of tomorrow. To add to the excitement, this year’s edition was held at the monumental Freedom Park in the historical suburbs of old Bangalore. What once was a central jail is now a public gathering space reserved for all things freedom and democracy.

![Freedom Park](/assets/ramblings/freedom.webp)

Past couple of days leading up to the event, India as a whole was plagued with a rather interesting problem: flight troubles. Not going too much into that (I have a rambling reserved for monopolies but that’s for another day), the LitFest was no different. Several speakers missed their shot to impress because of this, and I personally got to sit through two sessions lacking a speaker or two. One such session was on translations, presided over by Deepa Bhasthi, celebrated translator of Banu Mushtaq’s magnum opus The Heart Lamp and hosted by Keshava Guha. As someone who exclusively reads works from the author’s original writings, it was quite interesting to understand the nuances of translation. It is easy to take good translations for granted when one has seldom come across what would be described as a bad adaptation. The work that these folks do in terms of making publications more accessible often goes unrecognised. It is always great to see them get their flowers and to get the word across on what goes into adapting a publication.

![Talk](/assets/ramblings/translation.webp)

The LitFest had a good collection of books on sale, mostly featuring authors present at the event. While some people did get a shot at getting an autograph from their favourite authors, I unfortunately did not, owing to my packed schedule on the day. However, I managed to snag myself a copy of _Bangalore Through the Centuries_, an account of how the city has evolved over the years and how it shapes the lives of its denizens. The book walks us through the founding of Bengaluru, debunking popular urban legends of the city being a boiled bean lover’s haven. It also mentions the four watchtowers of Bengaluru, which yours truly managed to _conquer_ during the lockdown. I also happened to learn the historical significance of Bengaluru during the great Mysore wars and its growth into predominance during the British Raj. It explores how Bengaluru is a hotpot of several cultures that have shaped the lives of its inhabitants, be it the Bengaluru Karaga, the temples of old Bengaluru, or the Trinity Church. Although the book needs a few more chapters since its publication in the 1970s, it was a very fun and insightful read. I wholeheartedly recommend it, hopefully it adds _salubrious_ and _hitherto_ to your vocabulary as well.

![Book](/assets/ramblings/book.webp)

All in all, the LitFest was an eventful weekend packed with fun sessions, literature trivia, insightful talks, and of course, a spending spree on books to sustain me for a year. And as always, I look forward to next year’s LitFest.
